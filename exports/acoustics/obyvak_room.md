# Obývák ceiling — living-room recommendation

This is not a concert hall and not a single RT60. The question is what ceiling a person hears in this living room, from 250 Hz up. 125 Hz and below is not a result of this model: the Schroeder frequency for this volume (about 225 m³) sits near 100–150 Hz when the decay is half a second to a second, and ray energy is not a modal model. Bass modes are the next piece of work, not a number in this note.

The shell is the as-built air (plan 5350 × 11100 mm, both slopes, the vertical soffit bulkhead at the cabinet line, both bass-trap faces). Wool thickness does not remesh it. A ray that hits a surface takes α at that arrival angle from an extended-reaction transfer matrix (Miki 1990 for the porous layers only). The statistical (Paris) coefficient is in the CSV so it can be compared. The rays do not use it, and they do not use normal incidence.

Sources, the same for every construction. Receivers are not on the sources.

4000 rays, seed 4100 + seat index, ISO 9613-1 air at 20 °C and 50 % RH. Untreated faces (plaster, glass, floor, cabinets, trap bottoms) are α = 0.03, a typical mid-band figure, identical in every case, not a measurement of this room. Without that, specular rays that never meet the ceiling never decay and T20 is just the bounce cutoff.

## What to build

Build the ceiling that is already drawn: 60 mm naturheld 140, 40 mm Flex, battens, foil, gypsum. Duct clearance on that pack is +7 mm. It is buildable. Do not move the ducts to fit a thicker pack.

Nothing buildable that still has a porous face changes the mid-band decay by enough to hear, once the uncertainty is admitted. 40 mm of 140 with 40 mm of Flex (clearance +27 mm) and 40 mm of 140 with 60 mm of Flex (clearance +7 mm) sit on top of the as-built early decay. The unbuildable 60/60 cell (clearance −13 mm) does too, and it is not a recommendation. Raising the 140 from the declared minimum 60 kPa·s/m² to the 75 analogue, on the as-built slope only, does not move the seats either.

Do not strip both Naturheld products. Foil and gypsum alone leave a room that is too live for speech and ordinary listening: the early decay (EDT) goes from about 0.9–1.1 s to about 2.6–2.8 s at every seat, at both 500 and 1000 Hz. That gap is larger than the uncertainty.

Removing only the Flex and the battens does not do that. The 140 remains, the open fraction goes to 1 (no timber), and the early decay stays within about a tenth of a second of the as-built room. The gypsum does not come down with the latě: the CD grid and the hangers carry it, so nothing has to replace the battens. There is still no acoustic reason to take the Flex out. At 500 Hz the 140 (σ = 60 kPa·s/m²) is outside the Miki window; the Flex at 5 kPa·s/m² is inside it. The drawn stack is the one that is not only an extrapolation.

Removing only the 140 and keeping the Flex is also still a living room at 1000 Hz (inside the Miki window): EDT stays within about 0.05 s of as-built. At 500 Hz the kitchen and the gable seat lengthen by about 0.10–0.15 s. That is not "too live". It is also not an improvement. The plaster coat is on the 140, so it would go with the board.

The soffit bulkhead (about 6.9 m², fixed as-built stack) does absorb, but it is not the lever. At 1000 Hz its Paris absorption area is about 5 m² against about 49 m² on the slopes. The bass traps, as drawn, are sealed gypsum membranes over an air gap and wool. Their Paris α at 500–1000 Hz is about 0.001. They are in the sum, with angle-dependent α, and they do not absorb the mid band. Their resonance is in the bass, which this ray model does not claim. The wool in both traps is the Flex table value for that thickness (6 kPa·s/m² at 80 mm and at 300 mm), labelled as an assumption because the drawing says minerální vlna and names no product. It is the same in every case.

So another 20 mm of wool on the slope is not the lever, and neither is the soffit. The lever is having a porous ceiling at all.

## T20 at 500 and 1000 Hz

Seconds. Buildable options only, plus the gypsum-only case so the "too live" claim has a number. The 60/60 cell is omitted here because it is not buildable; it is in the CSV, marked, and it matches the as-built T20. These are the ray T20 values. They are a poor ranking tool in this room: after the ceiling has taken the first 10 dB, the late tail is the untreated surfaces at α = 0.03, so T20 clusters near 2.2–2.7 s for both a working ceiling and a bare gypsum ceiling. Read EDT below for what changes at a seat. Where T20 and T30 disagree by a second, the Schroeder fit has crossed a kink; that cell is not a different room.

Paris α of the slope at 1000 Hz is 0.74 as built, 0.74 at 40/40, 0.82 with the battens gone (the timber fraction was not absorbing), 0.59 with only the Flex, and 0.00 on foil and gypsum. Bass-trap Paris α is 0.001 or less. These Sabine numbers are shorter than T20 because Sabine has no late specular tail. They agree with the rays on the only ranking that matters: thickness does very little, and no wool at all does not.

## What is not trusted

- Miki 1990 is being used outside its window wherever f/σ < 0.01. For the 140 at the declared minimum 60 kPa·s/m² that is below about 600 Hz. The 500 Hz octave centre is still an extrapolation of that wool's impedance, even though a ray decay at 500 Hz is above the Schroeder frequency and is a geometric result. 1000 and 2000 Hz are inside the window. Flex at 5 or 6 kPa·s/m² is inside the window from 250 Hz up. The true 140 resistivity may be higher than 60; the 75 analogue does not change this recommendation.
- StoSilent Finish+Basic is acoustically transparent. That is an upper bound on absorption. No flow resistivity was invented for it. A real coat would make the room somewhat drier in these numbers than it will be. It would not make a bare gypsum ceiling acceptable, and it would not make 20 mm of extra wool suddenly matter.
- Bass is not modeled. The traps, as drawn, are membranes. Do not read their mid-band α as a bass-trap design.
- Scattering is an assumption, only on battened faces, and only when the 625 mm spacing is comparable to the wavelength: exp(−½ ln(spacing/λ)²). About 0.84 at 1000 Hz. Hard faces stay specular. The late T20 is where that assumption shows up. The early-decay ranking does not depend on it.
- Untreated α = 0.03 is an assumption. A rug or a bookshelf would shorten the late tail. It was not swept, because it is the same in every case.
- T20 in this room is not a straight decay. A single cell at 4.3 s next to an EDT of 1.1 s is a fit across a kink, not a measured reverberation time.
- The normal-incidence table is not the basis of this recommendation.
